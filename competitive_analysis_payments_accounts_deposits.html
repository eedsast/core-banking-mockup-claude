8 platforms evaluated on two key architectural and commercial questions

Q1 — Payments Is Payments sold/architected as a separate module or bundled into core?

Mambu Cloud-native
SaaS · Composable banking
Fintechs, digital banks, EMIs, credit unions · 260+ clients, 65 countries
Q1 — Payments
Separate add-on module
Mambu Payments launched May 2025 via acquisition of Numeral. Independently licensed, modularly scalable. Customers can use core without Payments.
Q2 — Accounts vs Deposits
Merged in one module
Mambu's Deposits module covers current accounts, savings, wallets, and term deposits in a single configurable engine. Product type is a configuration parameter, not a separate service.
Q1 — Payments detail
Acquired Numeral (Dec 2024), launched Mambu Payments at Money20/20 Europe (May 2025)
Composable approach: payments, lending, deposits are independently licensable modules — customers choose their own path
Natively integrated with core — payment routing updates GL accounts and handles AML suspense accounts automatically
Supports SEPA CT/DD, UK Faster Payments, real-time payments. Existing customers (Western Union, Indexo Bank, BCB) already expanding to Payments
Pre-payments era: customers integrated third-party PSPs (Wise, Marqeta, Stripe) via Mambu ecosystem connectors
Q2 — Accounts vs Deposits detail
Single Deposits module covers: current accounts, savings, group savings, wallets, term/fixed deposits
Product differentiation via configuration — account type, interest rules, maturity logic, rollover are parameters, not separate microservices
Confirmed by Uno Bank implementation: launched with CASA and fixed deposit products on the same Deposits module before later adding mortgages
Islamic banking (Mudarabah, Wakalah) also handled within same module with profit-sharing configuration
💡
Your lesson: Mambu's merged Deposits module is a commercial simplification trade-off. It reduces implementation complexity and speeds time-to-market for customers who don't need the split. The risk: one change to the term deposit maturity engine could affect current account behaviour. For your SaaS targeting EMIs, splitting gives you a cleaner licensing lever — EMIs get DDA only.
Thought Machine Cloud-native
Vault Core + Vault Payments · SaaS / self-hosted
Tier 1–3 banks, fintechs · JPMorgan Chase, Lloyds, Standard Chartered, Intesa Sanpaolo
Q1 — Payments
Fully separate product
Vault Payments is a completely separate product from Vault Core. Sold independently, deployed independently, can integrate with any core — not just Vault Core.
Q2 — Accounts vs Deposits
Universal product engine
Vault Core uses a single Universal Product Engine and Ledger. All product types (current, savings, deposits, loans) are smart contracts on the same platform. No separate microservices per product type.
Q1 — Payments detail
Vault Payments is a standalone cloud-native payment processing platform — built from scratch separately from Vault Core
Can be adopted as a standalone product integrating with any legacy core banking system — not just Vault Core
Supports ISO 20022 natively, card issuing on Mastercard network, all payment types and schemes via rails-based configuration
Vault Payments routes payments end-to-end, completely decoupling payment instrument from funding source — dynamic account routing
Gartner Magic Quadrant Leader 2025 for Retail Core Banking — recognised for both products separately
Q2 — Accounts vs Deposits detail
Philosophy: product engine is intentionally product-agnostic — does not "know" the product it runs. All products are smart contracts
Current accounts, savings, time deposits, credit cards, mortgages all run on the same Universal Product Engine and single Ledger
Product Library contains 200+ pre-written smart contracts including deposits and current accounts — banks customise before launch
Event sourcing: all account changes captured as immutable events on the same Ledger regardless of product type
Differentiation via smart contract parameters, not microservice separation — sophisticated but requires developer capability
💡
Your lesson: Thought Machine validates the separate Payments product decisively — Vault Payments can even be sold to banks not using Vault Core. This is the strongest market signal that separate Payments is commercially correct. Their universal product engine approach is elegant but demands developer investment — smart contracts in Python for every product. Your configuration-driven approach may be simpler to operate for Fintech/EMI customers.

10x Banking Cloud-native
SuperCore · Meta Core · SaaS (AWS)
Tier 1–2 banks, building societies, BaaS · Chase UK, Westpac, Old Mutual
Q1 — Payments
Integrated + partner ecosystem
10x does not have a native payments engine. Payments are delivered via pre-integrated partners (Paymentology for cards). The SuperCore platform integrates with external payment schemes via SmartAdapters. No standalone Payments module sold.
Q2 — Accounts vs Deposits
Single product engine — no split
SuperCore's ProductKit handles deposits and lending products across retail, SME and corporate in a single configurable platform. No microservice separation between account types — product type is a configuration, not a service boundary.
Q1 — Payments detail
SuperCore integrates with payment schemes via SmartAdapters — not a payments engine itself
Partnership with Paymentology: card issuing and processing on Visa/Mastercard and domestic switches — pre-integrated, single API
"Meta Core" philosophy: 10x focuses on the core ledger and product engine — payments are deliberately left to specialist partners
Kafka-based event streaming (Confluent Cloud) enables real-time data flow to payment partners without tight coupling
ISO 20022 message formats supported via partner integrations — not natively in the core
Q2 — Accounts vs Deposits detail
ProductKit abstracts complexity of building "deposit and lending products across retail, SME and corporate" — treated as one domain
Pre-built modules cover savings, deposits, current accounts — all configurable on the same SuperCore engine
Polyglot development environment — banks can customise at any level in any language, but product boundary is one engine
Chase UK and Westpac use SuperCore — both have term deposits and current accounts running on same platform instance
💡
Your lesson: 10x's "meta core" strategy deliberately avoids building a payments engine — they partner instead. This is a valid product strategy: be the best ledger and product engine, outsource payments to specialists. The risk is dependency on partners. For your product, owning Payments as a separately licensed module gives you more commercial control than a partnership model.

Q1 — Market verdict on Payments

6 out of 8 platforms treat Payments as architecturally separate. 5 of those sell or licence it independently. The industry has converged on separation.
The two exceptions (FLEXCUBE, Vodeno/Solarisbank) are either legacy bundle models or BaaS operators with a bank licence — different commercial motions entirely.
Thought Machine's Vault Payments selling to non-Vault-Core banks is the most powerful signal: Payments is a standalone market, not just a feature.
Your decision to sell Payments separately is correct and validated by every relevant cloud-native competitor in your target market.

Q2 — Market verdict on Accounts vs Deposits

7 of 8 platforms merge accounts and deposits in one module or service. Only Vodeno shows any catalogue-level separation (and even that may be presentational).
The industry convention — driven by legacy CASA heritage and implementation simplicity — is to keep them together.
Your split decision is architecturally correct and technically superior. It is not yet the industry norm, which means it is a differentiation opportunity, not a risk.
