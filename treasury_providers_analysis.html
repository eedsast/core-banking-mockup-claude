EMI Light Treasury — Provider Analysis

15 providers and routes for the Treasury module · scoped to an electronic money institution, not to a bank balance sheet

Why an EMI treasury is not a small bank treasury

An EMI has no deposit book, no IRRBB and no liquidity ratio. It has a safeguarding obligation, a currency mismatch and three balances it cannot spend — and the tooling built for the first problem does not fit the second.
Since 9 Oct 2025
Euro-area PSPs offering credit transfers must also send instant payments. Liquidity is consumed continuously, including nights and weekends, against a funding cycle that is not.
Since 17 Jan 2025
DORA applies. A treasury platform is an ICT third-party service: register of information, contractual terms, exit strategy and testing — all before the first position is loaded.
Adoption 2026
PSD3 and the PSR reached provisional political agreement in November 2025, with final compromise texts in April 2026. EMIs are re-authorised as payment institutions and safeguarding arrangements sit squarely in scope.
Likely late 2027
PSD3 and the PSR take effect, with transitional licensing provisions and EBA Level 2 standards to follow. Where safeguarded funds may sit is a Level 2 question with no answer yet.

How to read this analysis
An EMI treasury looks nothing like a bank treasury, and most treasury software is built for the latter
The EMI job list
Safeguard · Position · FX · Fund · Reconcile
1. Safeguarding adequacy — safeguarded assets at least equal to e-money outstanding, proven daily and per currency. 2. Position keeping — what is held where, and how much of it is actually free. 3. FX exposure — the mismatch between the currency owed and the currency held. 4. Funding — card prefund, settlement cover, instant-payments buffer. 5. Reconciliation — nostro, scheme and safeguarding breaks.
What is not on the list
No IRRBB, no LCR, no deposit modelling
An EMI does not lend its float, does not run a maturity transformation book and is not subject to the CRR liquidity regime. Gap analysis, EVE sensitivity, behavioural deposit modelling and funds transfer pricing are all absent. Anything sold as "bank ALM" is answering a question the institution does not have — and charging for it.
💡
The central decision for your Treasury module: the deliverable is a restriction-aware, currency-by-currency position with a forward schedule attached. That single read model answers the safeguarding test, the FX exposure question and the free-cash question at once. Everything else in this document is an adapter onto it — and the heavyweight ALM market is simply out of scope until a customer holds a banking licence.

The safeguarding position

Not a vendor category. The two constraints that decide what every other section is allowed to do.
What an EMI may do with the float Constraint
Permitted investments · currency matching
The question that decides whether an investment module is worth building at all
Q1 — Can safeguarded funds be invested?
Yes — from a narrow, nationally defined list
Investment is one of the two safeguarding methods under Article 10 PSD2 and Article 7 EMD2 — not an exception to safeguarding. The permitted universe is narrow and set nationally. The Bank of Lithuania defines secure, liquid low-risk assets as cash and cash equivalents; bonds issued by a government, central bank or company with a long-term rating of at least BBB, exchange-traded with prices always available; short-term deposits of up to one year at credit institutions; and collective investment schemes investing only in those. The CSSF frames the same idea by capital treatment — a specific risk requirement no greater than 1.6% — plus UCITS units investing solely in such assets.
Q2 — Is FX hedging the real obligation?
Separate obligation, not an alternative
Currency matching is a safeguarding adequacy question. CSSF Circular 26/906 recommends holding safeguarded funds in the currency of the funds owed to users, and requires additional protection where they are held in another currency. A multi-currency EMI must test its safeguarding position currency by currency, not as one converted total — so investing and managing the open position are two duties that both apply.
The worked example that makes it concrete
A EUR safeguarding account covering USD e-money fell roughly USD 0.81m short — about 2% — purely because the euro weakened. No payment moved and no customer did anything; the institution was simply under-safeguarded by the close
That is a breach produced by an exchange rate, which is why converting everything to a reporting currency hides exactly the risk the regulator is testing
The mitigation named in supervisory text is "additional protection" rather than any specific instrument — so the control is the measurement, and hedging is one way to act on it
Own funds are a different pool entirely: capital and retained earnings are not safeguarded and are not bound by this list
Implications for your platform
Model the safeguarding test per currency as a first-class daily output — assets held against e-money outstanding, each currency standing alone, with the shortfall or surplus stated
The permitted-investment universe is set nationally and differs in form between regulators, so it is configuration with an effective date, never a constant in code
PSD3 and the PSR revisit safeguarding with the detail deferred to EBA Level 2 standards that do not yet exist — treat today's list as a version, not a fact
Eligibility enforcement belongs in the platform: an instruction to place funds in an ineligible asset should fail before it reaches a venue, not be caught in an audit
💡
Fit: both duties are real, and the measurement is the product. An EMI can invest its float, within a list narrow enough that the investment decision is almost mechanical once eligibility and free balance are known. It must also manage the currency mismatch, because an unhedged position can breach safeguarding with no transaction at all. Knowing how much is free, in which currency, under which national list — that is the hard part, and it sits in your core rather than in any vendor's.
The locked positions Cross-cutting
Card prefund · safeguarded float · instant-payments buffer
The three largest balances in an EMI, and the reason generic treasury tools mislead
Q1 — What is actually happening
Most of the cash is not available
A card programme requires a prefund account held by the BIN sponsor as security against expected transactions, which the sponsor may draw on for scheme liabilities and fees. Safeguarded customer funds are segregated and restricted by regime. An instant-payments buffer must cover 24/7 outflow against a funding cycle that pauses. All three are cash on the balance sheet that the treasurer cannot deploy.
Q2 — Why generic tools get it wrong
They model one entity's own cash
A corporate treasury system treats a bank balance as spendable. In a regulated payments business the headline balance is mostly other people's money or collateral, so free cash is a computed figure rather than a sum of accounts — and it is the only figure an investment decision may use.
What the position model needs
A restriction type on every balance — own funds, safeguarded, prefund collateral, pledged, in settlement — so free cash is derived rather than assumed
Forward schedules from the loan book: disbursements committed, collections expected, with the arrears assumption visible and adjustable
Scheme settlement obligations by value date, since prefund sizing is driven by card volume and its volatility
A weekend and holiday calendar per rail and counterparty — the instant-payments buffer is a weekend problem before it is a weekday one
Implications for your platform
This is the module's actual product. Everything else in this document is a vendor; the restriction-aware, per-currency position is the part only your core can assemble
Prefund sizing is a recurring commercial negotiation with the BIN sponsor — evidencing the right number with data saves the customer real money
The same model answers the supervisory question on safeguarding adequacy, which makes it two features from one build
History matters as much as the current figure: "what was free cash on the day we placed that deposit" is an audit question
💡
Fit: the reason this module cannot simply be bought. Every vendor here assumes someone hands it a clean, correctly classified position. In an EMI nobody does, because the balances sit across cards, payments, lending and safeguarding. Assembling and classifying them is the work, and your platform is the only component positioned to do it.

FX and the open position

For an EMI this is a safeguarding control before it is a treasury activity.
Currencycloud, Ebury, Convera, Nium FX execution
Conversion, forwards, multi-currency collection and payout
Any EMI holding liabilities in more than one currency — which is most of them
Q1 — Coverage & capability
Execution and reach, not measurement
Spot conversion, forward contracts where the provider is licensed to offer them, multi-currency accounts and payout reach. Currencycloud sits inside Visa; Ebury and Convera carry deeper corporate hedging books. They execute against a position someone else has calculated — none of them will tell you what your safeguarding mismatch is.
Q2 — Integration & commercial fit
Spread-based, relationship-gated
Economics sit in the spread rather than in a licence fee, so the comparison is rate quality and reach rather than subscription cost. Forward contracts may require separate permissions and credit assessment, which is a lead time to plan for rather than an API to call.
What the module must decide
Match first, hedge second. Holding safeguarded assets in the currency owed removes the exposure outright; hedging is what remains once matching is impractical
A mismatch tolerance per currency, with a breach alert — the control is continuous measurement, not a monthly review
Who bears the conversion spread on a customer transaction, since that answer decides whether FX is a cost line or a revenue line
Hedge accounting is almost certainly out of scope at this size; if a customer asks, that is a sign they need more than a light module
Implications for your platform
The FX providers are covered in depth in the FX and remittance analysis; treasury consumes the rate and reports the resulting position
Keep execution behind an interface — rate request, deal capture, settlement instruction — so the venue is swappable per corridor
Record the rate, the timestamp and the counterparty on every conversion; a safeguarding position reconstructed later needs the rate that applied, not today's
For the Egyptian corridor, currency controls and availability change the calculus entirely — treat EGP exposure as a separate problem, not a line in a table
💡
Fit: buy execution, own the exposure number. The open position is a figure only your platform can calculate, because it needs e-money outstanding by currency on one side and safeguarded assets by currency on the other. Once that number exists, acting on it is a phone call or an API call to any of four competent providers.

Investment execution

TreasurySpring, Flagstone, MMF portals Investment venue
Fixed-term funds · deposit marketplaces · money market fund access
An EMI with a float it is permitted to place, and own funds it is free to place
Q1 — Coverage & capability
Access and diversification, as a product
TreasurySpring offers fixed-term fund products across eight currencies through a single digital onboarding, with a public API and over $350bn in cumulative flows. Flagstone is a deposit marketplace spreading cash across many banks under one onboarding. ICD and Calastone are the traditional money market fund portals. Short-term bank deposits and qualifying collective schemes are both inside the permitted list — which is what makes this section viable rather than theoretical.
Q2 — Integration & commercial fit
No licence cost; economics in the yield
Venues rather than software subscriptions, so the conversation is yield, term and counterparty diversification rather than seats. TreasurySpring's public API and existing Atlar integration make it the straightforward one to automate against.
Why it belongs in the module
Single-counterparty concentration is a finding waiting to happen. One onboarding that spreads cash across many issuers answers it structurally rather than operationally
A maturity ladder inside the one-year deposit limit is the lightest possible liquidity management, and it is scheduling — which your platform already does well
Yield on the float is a revenue line for the institution, which makes this the part of the treasury module that pays for the rest of it
Own funds carry none of the safeguarding restrictions, so there are two pools with two rule sets and the platform should keep them visibly apart
The constraints to enforce
Eligibility is nationally defined and expressed differently by each regulator — a ratings floor and instrument list in one, a capital-treatment threshold in another. Same intent, different test
Liquidity is part of the test, not a preference: a term that cannot be broken to meet redemptions defeats the purpose of safeguarding
Placement must not create a currency mismatch — an eligible asset in the wrong currency is a safeguarding problem wearing an investment label
PSD3 and the PSR revisit all of this with detail deferred to EBA Level 2 standards, so build the rule set as dated configuration
💡
Fit: build the ladder and the eligibility rule; buy the venue. Placement is an API call. The product value is in knowing how much is genuinely free, in which currency, for how long, under which national list — a question only something holding the full position and the full forward schedule can answer.

Counterparties and reconciliation

The slowest dependency and the cheapest adapter. Neither is optional.
Banking Circle, LHV, ClearBank Balance sheet
Settlement accounts · safeguarding accounts · clearing access
Every EMI, whether or not it buys any treasury software at all
Q1 — Coverage & capability
Accounts and rails, not analytics
Banking Circle is a wholesale bank for payments with multi-currency accounts, settlement, FX and recently stablecoin settlement services. LHV is the default EEA fintech banking partner with EUR clearing. ClearBank provides GBP and EUR accounts, safeguarding and interest-bearing balances. The safeguarding account itself lives here, which makes this the one relationship an EMI cannot operate without.
Q2 — Integration & commercial fit
A relationship, not a subscription
Onboarding is a credit and compliance process measured in months, pricing is relationship-based, and switching is a project. The slowest-moving dependency in the stack and the one to start earliest — before any software decision is made.
What the treasury module must model
Settlement timing per rail — when funds are actually available rather than when the message cleared, which differs by counterparty and currency
Cut-off times and the fate of a payment that misses one; a forecast ignoring cut-offs is wrong every afternoon
Which accounts are safeguarding accounts and which are operational, because the distinction drives the daily adequacy test
Per-counterparty concentration, since this is also where the institution's largest single credit exposure usually sits
Implications for your platform
The same counterparties appear in the payments and cards analyses. Model them once, as counterparties with accounts, restrictions and limits
Instant payments run outside banking hours against counterparties whose funding windows do not; size that gap explicitly rather than discovering it
A safeguarding account's acknowledgement letter and its operational terms are documents the platform should hold, not just facts it assumes
Onboarding lead times belong in the customer's deployment plan, not in your integration estimate
💡
Fit: the dependency to start before anything else. No treasury software compensates for a missing safeguarding relationship, and nothing in the stack takes longer to put in place. For a Phase 1 customer the sequence is counterparty first, position view second, everything else after.

Matching at volume, with audit evidence
Rules-based and increasingly model-assisted matching across statements, scheme settlement files and internal ledgers, with break management, ageing and evidence for audit. AutoRek and Kani are the payments- and card-native options; Duco and SmartStream are the broader enterprise platforms.
Q2 — Integration & commercial fit
File-driven, easy to adapt
Feeds in, breaks out — one of the easier adapters in the whole platform. Kani is priced for fintech volumes; the enterprise names assume enterprise reconciliation estates and enterprise budgets.
Why it is a treasury problem
An unreconciled position is not a position. The safeguarding test assumes the balances are true, and reconciliation is what makes them true
Safeguarding reconciliation carries its own supervisory expectation and its own frequency, distinct from ordinary nostro matching
Card scheme settlement is a daily file-driven reconciliation against the prefund account — a treasury position, not just an operations task

Implications for your platform
Expose a clean ledger extract per account and per day; that single interface serves any of these vendors
Breaks belong in the same case queue as the compliance alerts from the onboarding module, with the same SLA machinery
A position that cannot show its unreconciled balance separately overstates certainty; make the distinction visible on the screen
Basic matching is buildable; exception management, ageing and audit evidence are where the bought products earn their price
💡
Fit: buy it, and feed it well. The cheapest adapter in the treasury scope and the one that most improves the credibility of everything else. A position labelled with what is confirmed and what is still matching is worth more to a treasurer than a single confident number.

Q1 — Coverage & capability verdict

An EMI can invest its float, from a narrow list. Investing is one of the two safeguarding methods under Article 10 PSD2 and Article 7 EMD2, not an exception to safeguarding: cash and cash equivalents, investment-grade exchange-traded bonds with available prices, short-term credit institution deposits up to one year, and collective schemes holding only those. The list is set nationally and expressed differently by each regulator, so it is dated configuration rather than a constant.
FX management is a separate duty, not an alternative to investing. Safeguarded assets should sit in the currency owed, and where they do not, additional protection is required. The position is tested currency by currency rather than as a converted total — a EUR account covering USD e-money fell about 2% short on an exchange rate move alone, with no payment and no customer action.
Nothing in the market models safeguarding. Cash platforms show untyped balances, which means the free-cash figure they display is the one that overstates. The restriction typing, the per-currency test and the forward schedule all have to come from the core.
Bank ALM is the wrong purchase at this licence. IRRBB, EVE sensitivity, liquidity ratios and behavioural deposit modelling answer a regime an EMI is not in. Naming that boundary plainly is worth more in a pitch than implying coverage of it.

Q2 — What this means for the module

Build one read model, buy five adapters. Restriction-typed balances per currency, plus committed and expected flows by value date, answers the safeguarding test, the FX exposure and the free-cash question at once. Connectivity, FX execution, placement, reconciliation and any future ALM all hang off it.
Sequence by lead time. Safeguarding and settlement counterparty onboarding takes months and blocks everything; the position view runs in parallel; FX, placement and reconciliation follow. A plan that starts with software selection stalls at the first credit committee.
Two conversations to open now: TreasurySpring, as an API-addressable venue inside the permitted universe that turns eligible surplus into a revenue line, and Atlar, as both the product benchmark for the screen and a likely incumbent at prospects.
The eligibility rule is the feature, not the placement. Enforcing which assets, in which currency, for how long, against today's free balance — and refusing an ineligible instruction before it reaches a venue — is what an EMI treasurer cannot get from a cash platform, and what PSD3's unfinished Level 2 standards make worth parameterising now.
