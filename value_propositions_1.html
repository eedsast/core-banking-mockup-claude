Value Propositions — Core Banking Scope & Strategic Direction

21 candidate propositions, tiered MUST → differentiators → game changers · scope decision per proposition

Our path

The Bank-in-a-Box — API-first, cloud-native, licence-aware.

One platform that takes a licensed EMI, PI or small bank from authorisation to live product in weeks, not quarters: accounts, cards, payments, onboarding and the business layer above them, delivered as independently licensable modules on a single cloud-native core. Not a ledger we expect the customer to assemble a bank around, and not a BaaS that absorbs their licence — a box they own, run under their own licence, and grow out of module by module.

Bank in a box
Everything a licensed institution needs to operate on day one — ledger, products, payments, onboarding, back office, customer channels — pre-wired and configured, not integrated by the customer. Launch-ready, not construction-ready.
API first
Every capability is an API before it is a screen. The back office and white-label front ends are the platform's own first clients, which guarantees the API is complete rather than an afterthought bolted beside a UI.
Cloud native
Containerised, horizontally scalable, event-sourced, deployable as multi-tenant SaaS or in the customer's own EU cloud tenancy for data-residency and DORA exit-plan requirements. No on-premise variant.
Compete above the ledger. The functional floor is settled across the market. The defensible ground is the business layer: onboarding, invoicing, receivables, embedded credit, loyalty, AI-assisted operations.
Licence-aware by design. An EMI cannot take deposits or lend. Module boundaries follow the regulatory perimeter, so customers buy what their licence permits and upgrade when it changes.
Sponsorship is a stage, not a state. Carry the customer from a sponsored BIN and pooled vIBANs to their own BIC and direct scheme participation without re-platforming.
Orchestrate, never rebuild. Cards, KYC, screening, FX, credit bureau and trade finance are adapters behind our own APIs. We own the ledger, the products and the workflow.
Purpose Define the functional scope of the Core Banking platform by deciding which value propositions are built, when, and why — before architecture commits.
Method Baseline against what standard providers ship (Mambu, Tuum, Thought Machine, Solaris, Vodeno, Swan, Paynetics, Temenos, FLEXCUBE, Finacle), then add propositions the incumbents do not package.
Market baseline
What "standard" means in 2026
Derived from the 8-platform competitive analysis plus EU BaaS/EMI infrastructure providers
What everyone ships
Commoditised
Ledger, multi-currency accounts, IBAN, card issuing hook, SEPA/SCT Inst, FX, fees and limits, KYC workflow, API-first delivery. None of this differentiates — its absence disqualifies.
Where the white space is
Open ground
Business-side propositions (invoicing, receivables, marketplace payouts, loyalty, embedded credit, trade finance) and AI-assisted operations sit above the core at every incumbent — bolted on by the customer, not sold by the platform.
💡
Strategic frame: the core banking market has converged on the same functional floor. Competing on ledger quality is a losing game against Thought Machine and Temenos. The defensible position for a Fintech/EMI-focused SaaS is owning the layer the EMI's own customers actually pay for — onboarding, invoicing, collections, credit, loyalty — delivered as licensable modules on a core that EMIs can run inside their own licence perimeter.
Tier 1 · MUST

Non-negotiable

The platform is not sellable without these. No differentiation, total disqualification risk.
IBAN Account MUST
Accounts · Core ledger
Every EMI, PI, neobank and embedded-finance brand
Customer value
Addressable money
A real, reachable IBAN per end customer — multi-currency, with virtual IBANs underneath for segregation and reconciliation. This is the product the EMI's own customers think they are buying.
Commercial value
Per-account subscription
The volume driver of the whole licence: priced per active account per month. Everything else attaches to it. Also the anchor for the demand-account / term-deposit split you have already decided.
Functional scope
Account hierarchy: master / sub-account / virtual IBAN, with per-level balance and statement
Multi-currency balances on one customer, with FX booking against a configurable rate source
Holds, authorisations, available vs booked balance, value dating, back-dated corrections
Statement, audit trail and GL posting per movement — immutable, event-sourced
Dependencies & build notes
IBAN issuance depends on the sponsor (see Payment Processor Analysis) — the platform must support both sponsored IBAN ranges and the customer's own range
Must model e-money (EMI) and deposit (credit institution) semantics differently — safeguarding vs deposit-taking is a hard regulatory boundary
IBAN portability between sponsors is the single biggest migration pain point — design for sponsor swap from day one
Verification of Payee (VoP) must be answerable on inbound checks
💡
Scope decision: MVP — core. Build sponsor-agnostic. The commercial differentiator is not the account, it is the ability to re-point an EMI's IBAN range from one sponsor to another without re-platforming. No incumbent markets that capability; every EMI that lived through the PayrNet revocation understands it.
Digital wallet MUST
Accounts · E-money balance
EMIs, wallet operators, gig and marketplace platforms, closed-loop schemes
Customer value
Low-friction balance
A stored-value balance that can be opened with light KYC, funded by card or transfer, spent instantly, and upgraded to a full IBAN Account when thresholds are hit.
Commercial value
Entry-tier licence
The cheapest way for a prospect to go live. Wallet-only pricing creates a low-friction land, with the IBAN and Payments modules as the natural expand.
Functional scope
Tiered KYC: anonymous / simplified / full, with limits enforced per tier and automatic step-up prompts
Top-up by card, SCT Inst, direct debit, voucher; payout back to source for refunds
P2P transfer inside the scheme by alias (phone, email, handle) — not IBAN
Multi-wallet per customer: spending, savings pot, merchant settlement, escrow
Dependencies & build notes
Alias directory is the hidden complexity — needs to be its own service, not a column on the account
E-money redemption rights and safeguarding reporting must be first-class, not bolted on
Shares the ledger with the IBAN Account — the difference is product configuration and limit policy, not a separate ledger
Closed-loop / open-loop toggle changes the AML model significantly
💡
Scope decision: MVP — core. Wallet and IBAN Account should be the same ledger with different product policies. This is the one place where the industry's "merge" instinct is correct: both are demand accounts. Keep the split you planned between demand (wallet + IBAN Account) and term (deposits) — not between wallet and account.
Onboarding & KYC MUST
Customer lifecycle · KYC / KYB / AML
Every institution, every customer type — the gate everything else sits behind
Customer value
Minutes to first transaction
A configurable onboarding journey — individual and company — that identifies, screens, risk-rates and opens the account without a human unless the risk model asks for one. Conversion at onboarding is the single biggest driver of a fintech's acquisition cost.
Commercial value
Licensable module, provider-agnostic
Priced per onboarded customer or per journey, with provider costs passed through. The platform's value is the orchestration and the audit trail, not the identity check itself — which is why it must never be welded to one vendor.
Functional scope
Journey engine: configurable step sequence per product, risk tier, country and customer type, with save-and-resume and abandonment recovery
KYC: document capture, NFC chip read, liveness and biometric match, PEP and sanctions screening, adverse media, address and phone verification
KYB: registry lookup, company structure, UBO discovery and verification through ownership chains, director screening, document collection
Risk scoring and customer risk rating, with the rating driving limits, monitoring frequency and review cycle
Perpetual KYC: scheduled refresh, event-triggered re-review, ongoing screening, discrepancy reporting
Case management for manual review, four-eyes approval, refusal with reason codes, and a complete decision audit trail
Dependencies & build notes
Orchestrate, do not build. Identity verification, screening and registry data are vendor markets with real moats. Build the journey, the risk model, the case management and the audit trail
Multi-provider by design: customers arrive with contracts, and fallback routing between providers raises pass rates materially — see the Onboarding KYC/KYB Analysis
AMLR applies from 10 July 2027 with a harmonised CDD regime, annual refresh for higher-risk customers and five-yearly for others, and a requirement for meaningful human intervention in onboarding and refusal decisions
eIDAS 2.0: member states must offer an EU Digital Identity Wallet by end-2026, and relying parties must accept it when presented from end-2027 — the journey engine needs a wallet path alongside the document path
KYB is the harder half and the one most platforms neglect; it is also what the Business Account proposition depends on
💡
Scope decision: MVP — core module, separately licensable. Promoted out of Platform services because it is a product in its own right, with its own buyer (the compliance function), its own vendor ecosystem and its own regulatory calendar. Two deadlines make it the most time-sensitive item in this document: the EUDI Wallet acceptance obligation and the AMLR application date. A platform whose journey engine already speaks both is selling readiness its competitors will be retrofitting.
Cards MUST
Issuing · Virtual + physical
Every consumer and business proposition in the EEA
Customer value
Spend anywhere
Virtual card in seconds, tokenised to Apple/Google Pay, physical card optional. Without a card the account is a parking space, not a product.
Commercial value
Licence + interchange share
Priced per active card. Optional upside: a revenue share on interchange where you intermediate the processor relationship — but that pulls you toward programme management, a different business.
Functional scope
Card lifecycle: issue, activate, freeze, replace, PIN, 3DS enrolment, tokenisation
Authorisation handling: real-time balance check, holds, partial reversals, clearing match, chargeback states
Spend controls: MCC, merchant, geography, channel, amount, velocity — per card and per group
Scheme settlement reconciliation and interchange/fee accrual into the GL
Dependencies & build notes
Do not build a card processor. Integrate to issuer-processors and BIN sponsors (see Card Processing Analysis)
Build a processor-abstraction layer: one internal card API, adapters per processor. Customers arrive with incumbent processor relationships
The authorisation path is the only sub-100ms requirement in the whole platform — architect it separately from batch posting
Credit/revolving cards are a different product (see Game changers) — do not conflate with debit issuing
💡
Scope decision: MVP — adapter only. Ship with two processors integrated at launch, abstracted behind one API. The value proposition is "bring your own processor, we normalise it", not "we are your processor". That is also what keeps you out of PCI scope.
Money Send MUST
Payments · Outbound orchestration
All segments · SEPA CT, internal, SWIFT, local rails
Customer value
One API, every rail
Outbound payment with Verification of Payee across SCT, internal book transfer, SWIFT and local rails, with one API, one status model and one set of exception semantics regardless of which rail carries it.
Commercial value
Separately licensed module
Confirmed by the competitive analysis: 6 of 8 platforms treat Payments as architecturally separate, 5 licence it independently. Price per transaction or per tier.
Functional scope
ISO 20022 native (pacs.008 / pacs.002 / pacs.004 / camt.05x) — you already have this schema designed
Smart routing: pick rail by cost, cut-off, amount, currency, beneficiary reachability
Verification of Payee on outbound, recall and return handling, R-transaction lifecycle
Batch and scheduled payments, standing orders, bulk payout files
Dependencies & build notes
Rail access via sponsor (CENTROlink, ClearBank, LHV, Banking Circle) or, since October 2025, direct participation in T2 / TIPS / RT1 / STEP2 as a non-bank PSP
Settlement, nostro/vostro and liquidity management are the hard part — not message formatting
Sanctions screening in the payment path, with a hold-and-release queue
Instant settlement specifics live in the Instant Payments card — same payment object, different rail and different SLA
💡
Scope decision: MVP — separate module, separately licensed. Your Q1 decision is correct and market-validated. Build the rail adapter so a customer can start on a sponsor and migrate to direct T2/TIPS participation without changing the application layer — that migration path is itself a sellable proposition.
Instant Payments MUST
Payments · SCT Inst · IPR-mandated
Every euro-area PSP — no longer a premium feature, a legal obligation
Customer value
10 seconds, 24/7/365
Send and receive euro credit transfers within ten seconds, every hour of every day, with Verification of Payee on the payer side and no price premium over a standard transfer. Instant is the new baseline user expectation, not a differentiator.
Commercial value
Compliance, not revenue
The Instant Payments Regulation caps charges at the level of an equivalent non-instant transfer, so this cannot be priced as a premium. It is sold as part of the Payments module and its absence loses the deal outright.
Functional scope
SCT Inst send and receive against TIPS or RT1, with ten-second end-to-end SLA and timeout/unknown-outcome handling
Verification of Payee as both requester and responder — the responder side is an inbound obligation on the account-holding PSP
24/7 operation: no nightly batch window, no end-of-day close that blocks posting, no maintenance outage that stops receipt
Real-time sanctions screening inside the ten-second budget, with a defined behaviour when screening cannot complete in time
Intraday liquidity monitoring on the settlement account, with funding thresholds and alerts
Dependencies & build notes
The architectural consequence is the point. 24/7 instant settlement forbids a batch-oriented ledger. Interest accrual, statement generation and reporting must run alongside live posting, not in a window where posting stops
Screening within ten seconds rules out synchronous human review; the risk model must pre-authorise, with post-event investigation
Mandatory SCT Inst reachability means every EMI needs either a sponsor with TIPS/RT1 access or direct participation — reachability is now a licensing-adjacent question, not a product choice
Legacy cores that cannot run 24/7 are being displaced by exactly this requirement — it is a migration trigger worth tracking in the pipeline
💡
Scope decision: MVP — core, inside the Payments module. Listed separately from Money Send because the obligation and the architecture are different: Money Send is routing, Instant Payments is an always-on, sub-ten-second, VoP-answering commitment that constrains the whole ledger design. Build it first and the rest of the payments estate inherits the discipline; retrofit it and the batch assumptions have to be unpicked everywhere.
Money Request MUST
Payments · Inbound initiation
P2P apps, SME accounts, marketplaces, invoicing flows
Customer value
Pull, not push
Request-to-Pay, payment links and QR: the payee initiates, the payer approves. Settles over SCT Inst with no card rails, no chargebacks and no interchange.
Commercial value
Bridge to merchant revenue
The feature that turns a consumer wallet into an acceptance product. It is the cheapest route for your customers into merchant monetisation without acquiring.
Functional scope
SEPA Request-to-Pay (SRTP) messaging, plus a proprietary in-scheme request for same-platform users
Payment link and QR generation with expiry, amount lock or payer-entered amount
Request states: pending, accepted, rejected, expired, partially paid — reconciled to the settling SCT Inst
Splitting: one request to many payers, with per-payer tracking
Dependencies & build notes
SRTP reach in the EEA is still thin — design so the proprietary in-scheme path carries the volume initially
Open-banking payment initiation (PIS) is the practical fallback for off-platform payers
Request objects must be first-class and linkable to an invoice (see Invoicing)
Dispute handling is contractual, not scheme-driven — set expectations in the product
💡
Scope decision: MVP — basic; Phase 2 — SRTP. Ship in-scheme requests and payment links in the MVP; add full SRTP when reach justifies it. Model the request as a durable object from day one so Invoicing and BNPL can both attach to it later.
Platform services & security MUST
Party · Pricing · GL · Security · Resilience
Non-negotiable foundations under every other proposition
Customer value
Invisible when right
Party model, limits, fee and pricing engine, general ledger, regulatory reporting, audit — plus the security and resilience posture the customer's own regulator will audit. Customers never ask for these by name; they walk away when they are weak.
Commercial value
Bundled, not priced
Include in the base licence. Attempting to price these separately reads as nickel-and-diming and loses deals against Tuum and Mambu, who bundle them. Security, however, is a sales asset — it survives procurement scrutiny or the deal dies there.
Functional scope
Party model: natural person, legal entity, beneficial ownership, group hierarchy, roles
Pricing engine: fee schedules, tiers, promotions, per-customer overrides, rebates
Double-entry GL with configurable chart of accounts, plus safeguarding reconciliation reporting
Regulatory reporting extract layer, with jurisdiction-specific mappings on top
Security & resilience — the audited surface
Tenant isolation and data segregation — demonstrable, not asserted. The first question every regulated prospect's auditor asks of a multi-tenant core
Encryption in transit and at rest, with customer-held keys where the customer's risk function requires it; field-level encryption and tokenisation for PII and card data
Identity and access: RBAC down to field level, SSO/SAML/OIDC, MFA mandatory for operator access, privileged-access review, segregation of duties enforced in the workflow
Immutable audit trail: who saw what, who changed what, who approved what — queryable, exportable, tamper-evident. This is what turns an audit from a project into a report
SCA and payment security: strong customer authentication, transaction signing, device binding, API rate limiting and anomaly detection on the API surface itself
DORA posture: ICT risk documentation, incident classification and reporting, tested business continuity and recovery objectives, penetration testing, and a written exit plan the customer can hand their supervisor
Certifications as table stakes: ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II and PCI DSS where card data is in scope. Absent these, enterprise procurement does not begin
💡
Scope decision: MVP — core, bundled; security treated as a product workstream, not a checklist. Two differentiators worth building deliberately. First, safeguarding-grade reconciliation: generalist cores built for banks treat it as a report, but for an EMI it is the most audited process in the business. Second, DORA-ready operational transparency: your customers are legally obliged to evidence ICT third-party risk, so every artefact you can hand them — architecture, incident history, exit plan, test results — removes work from their compliance function and from your own sales cycle.
Tier 2 · Differentiators

Where deals are won

Present at some competitors, poorly executed at most.
Business account Differentiator
Accounts · Multi-user, multi-entity
SME-focused EMIs, vertical SaaS embedding finance, corporate expense players
Customer value
Real company banking
Multiple users on one account with roles, approval thresholds, dual authorisation, entity hierarchies, per-department sub-accounts and cards, and an accounting export that reconciles.
Commercial value
Highest ARPU segment
Business accounts carry 5–20× the revenue per account of consumer. Most cloud cores model the retail customer first and retrofit business — a structural weakness you can design around.
Functional scope
Entity graph: parent company, subsidiaries, branches, each with own accounts and consolidated view
Role matrix: owner, admin, approver, initiator, viewer, accountant — with scoped permissions per account
Approval workflows: n-of-m authorisation, thresholds by amount/currency/beneficiary, four-eyes on beneficiary changes
Accounting integrations and a stable export schema (ledger lines, VAT fields, cost centres)
Dependencies & build notes
Depends entirely on the KYB half of Onboarding — UBO discovery, registry lookups, periodic re-verification
Retrofitting multi-user onto a single-customer party model is a rewrite. Decide now, not in Phase 2
Cost centres and VAT codes on transactions are what make the accounting export actually usable
Natural pairing with Expense management, Invoicing and Light Trade Finance — design them as one commercial bundle
💡
Scope decision: MVP — party model; Phase 1 — full feature. The party and permission model must be multi-user from the first line of code even if the UI ships later. This is the single highest-leverage architectural decision in the document, and the one that is impossible to reverse cheaply.
Expense management Differentiator
Cards · Spend control · Business
Corporate card programmes, SME neobanks, vertical SaaS
Customer value
Control before spend
Budgets per employee, team and project; receipt capture at the point of authorisation; policy enforcement in the auth path rather than in a monthly review.
Commercial value
Per-seat upsell
Priced per spending user, not per account. Converts a card programme from an interchange play into a SaaS revenue line for your customer — which is exactly the story that sells your module.
Functional scope
Budget objects with period, rollover, hard/soft limits, enforced at authorisation
Receipt capture, OCR, matching to authorisation, missing-receipt chasing
Policy engine: allowed MCCs per role, per-trip limits, out-of-policy flagging and approval
Single-use and vendor-locked virtual cards for subscriptions and procurement
Dependencies & build notes
Requires the business account party model and the card authorisation hook — no shortcut
Enforcement in the auth path means the policy engine must answer within the card timeout budget
Wallester, Enfuce and Pleo-class players have set the UX bar; parity is expected, not impressive
OCR is a buy, not a build
💡
Scope decision: Phase 2 — licensable module. High value, moderate build, clean module boundary. Worth noting that some card issuers (Wallester in particular) bundle expense management free with issuing — so position yours as the layer that works across whichever processor the customer already uses.
Collections & reconciliation Differentiator
Payments · Inbound · Receivables
PSPs, marketplaces, lenders, property and legal platforms, any EMI serving B2B
Customer value
Auto-matched money in
A dedicated virtual IBAN per payer, debtor, loan or invoice, so inbound funds reconcile themselves. Removes the single largest manual operations cost in a payments business.
Commercial value
Priced per vIBAN
Volume-based and sticky: once a customer's debtors are paying into your IBANs, switching cost is enormous. This is the highest-retention proposition on the list.
Functional scope
vIBAN issuance at scale with a reference model (per debtor, per invoice, per loan, per tenancy)
Matching engine: IBAN, reference, amount, name fuzzy-match, with a manual exception queue
SEPA Direct Debit: mandate management, pre-notification, R-transactions, representment
Automatic sweep from vIBAN to operating or client-money account, with full audit trail
Dependencies & build notes
vIBAN volume limits and naming rules are set by the sponsor — check before promising scale
Some sponsors require the vIBAN to be in the end customer's name, others pool — materially different AML treatment
The exception queue UI is the product. The matching algorithm is table stakes — and the first place AI earns its keep (see AI usage)
Direct debit is a credit-risk product, not just a message — model the collection-failure exposure
💡
Scope decision: Phase 1 — licensable module. This is the proposition with the best ratio of revenue to build effort, and the one that most directly differentiates a fintech-focused core from a bank core. Banks reconcile; fintechs need reconciliation as a product they resell.
Deposits & interest Differentiator
Accounts · Term · Separate module
Licensed credit institutions only — not EMIs
Customer value
Funding, not just flow
Savings, notice and term deposits with full maturity, rollover, early-break and interest-accrual logic, plus deposit-guarantee reporting.
Commercial value
The upsell lever
Your Q2 split exists for this reason: EMIs cannot take deposits, so they buy the core without this module. When a customer upgrades to a banking licence, the module is already built and waiting.
Functional scope
Term deposit lifecycle: booking, accrual, capitalisation, maturity, rollover, partial and full early break with penalty
Interest engine: tiered, stepped, promotional, bonus-on-condition, per-currency day-count conventions
Withholding tax calculation and reporting, per jurisdiction
Deposit guarantee scheme (DGS) eligibility flagging and single-customer-view extract
Dependencies & build notes
7 of 8 analysed platforms merge this with current accounts — your split is a genuine differentiator, but needs naming discipline against the industry's CASA convention
Shares the party and ledger services with the IBAN Account; owns its own product engine and scheduler
DGS reporting is a bank obligation with no EMI equivalent — a clean module boundary follows the regulation
Interest accrual at scale is a batch problem, but Instant Payments forbids a batch window — it must run concurrently with live posting
💡
Scope decision: Phase 2 — separate licensed module. Build it second, sell it to the minority of customers who can use it, and let its absence be a visible, priced-out line on every EMI quote. The licensing argument is the clearest commercial justification for the split, and no competitor currently uses it.
Tier 3 · Game changers

Not packaged by any analysed core banking vendor

Each one changes who you can sell to.
AI usage Game changer
Platform-wide · Migration · QA · Reconciliation · Fraud
Every customer, every phase — and the implementation team itself
Customer value
The work nobody wants to do
AI applied where banking actually burns money: migrating off a legacy core, testing what was migrated, clearing the reconciliation exception queue, and triaging fraud and AML alerts. Not a chatbot bolted to a dashboard — the four highest-cost manual processes in a payments business.
Commercial value
Margin on delivery + premium module
Two distinct returns. Internally, AI-assisted migration and QA cut your own implementation cost, which is the main thing standing between a fintech-tier price and a sustainable business. Externally, fraud and reconciliation intelligence are premium modules priced on outcome, not per seat.
Where it earns its keep
Migration — the biggest single lever. Legacy schema discovery and field mapping proposals, product-configuration inference from live data, transformation-rule generation, balance and position reconciliation between source and target, and automated triage of migration exceptions with a proposed fix per case. Migration cost is what kills core replacement deals; cutting it is a competitive weapon, not a convenience
QA and test. Test-case generation from specifications and from the API contract, synthetic but realistic test data across products and currencies, ISO 20022 message generation for every pacs/camt variant and edge case, regression-suite maintenance as the API evolves, and migration-parity testing that compares legacy and new output transaction by transaction
Reconciliation. Fuzzy matching on payer name, reference and amount where deterministic rules fail; ranked match suggestions in the exception queue; learning from analyst decisions so the queue shrinks over time; and anomaly detection on nostro, scheme settlement and safeguarding breaks before they become reportable
Fraud and AML management. Behavioural transaction monitoring rather than static rules, alert scoring to cut the false-positive rate that dominates compliance cost, narrative drafting for suspicious-activity reports with the analyst deciding, mule-network and structuring detection across accounts, and deepfake and injection-attack detection in the onboarding journey
Constraints that shape the design
Decision support, never decision maker. The AMLR requires meaningful human intervention in onboarding and refusal decisions, and the EU AI Act treats creditworthiness assessment as high-risk. Build AI as a ranked recommendation with a human approval step and a recorded rationale
Explainability is a product requirement, not a nice-to-have. Every AI-influenced decision needs a reason the customer's supervisor will accept. Model version, inputs, score, and the reviewer who approved it all belong in the audit trail
Data boundaries. No customer data leaves the tenancy or trains a shared model without explicit contractual permission. In a multi-tenant core this is the question every prospect's DPO will ask first
Start where errors are cheap and verifiable: migration mapping and test generation have a human reviewing every output anyway. Fraud scoring, where a false negative is a loss and a false positive is a lost customer, comes after the operational muscle exists
Buy the models, own the workflow, the feedback loop and the audit trail — the same orchestration principle as cards and KYC
💡
Scope decision: Phase 1 internally (migration + QA), Phase 2 externally (reconciliation + fraud). This is the most under-exploited item in the whole document. Every core banking vendor now claims AI; almost all of it points at the end customer's chat window. Pointing it instead at migration, testing, reconciliation and fraud attacks the four processes that make core banking expensive to buy and expensive to run — and the migration piece directly lowers the barrier to every deal you will ever pitch. Market it as evidence, not adjective: publish the measured reduction in migration effort and in false-positive rate.
Invoicing & e-invoicing Game changer
Business services · ViDA / Peppol
SME banking propositions, vertical SaaS, accounting-adjacent platforms
Customer value
Invoice → payment → ledger
Issue a compliant structured e-invoice, attach a Request-to-Pay and a dedicated vIBAN, and have the payment reconcile itself against the invoice. One object, one lifecycle.
Commercial value
New buyer, new budget
Moves you out of the core banking line item and into the business-software budget. It is also the gateway to receivables financing — the highest-margin product an SME platform can offer.
Functional scope
Structured e-invoice issuance and receipt (EN 16931 / Peppol BIS), with archival
Invoice object linked to a vIBAN, a Request-to-Pay and the settling transaction — automatic status transitions
Dunning: reminder schedules, escalation, partial payment, credit notes
Hook for financing: an unpaid invoice with a known payer is a collateralisable asset — the entry point to Light Trade Finance
Dependencies & build notes
ViDA was adopted in March 2025; national B2B mandates are already live or scheduled ahead of the EU-wide 2030 deadline (Italy since 2019, France and Spain well advanced)
Peppol access point: integrate a certified provider rather than becoming one
Country-specific tax field requirements are the long tail — build an extensible schema
Depends on Business account, Collections and Money Request
💡
Scope decision: Phase 2 — flagship module. One of the two strongest game-changers on the list. No analysed core banking vendor sells invoicing. Every EU SME will be compelled into structured e-invoicing by national mandates, and the platform that merges the invoice and the payment into one reconciled object owns the SME relationship.
Embedded credit — BNPL & revolving Game changer
Lending · Consumer & SME
Lenders, merchant platforms, card programmes moving beyond debit
Customer value
Revenue, not just float
Instalments on a card transaction, revolving credit lines, merchant-funded BNPL and SME working capital — originated, scored, serviced and collected inside the same platform.
Commercial value
Highest-value module
Credit is where your customers make real margin, which supports premium module pricing. It also plays directly to your existing loan management systems expertise.
Functional scope
Product engine: instalment plans, revolving lines with minimum payment, grace periods, interest and fee accrual
Origination: creditworthiness assessment workflow, affordability data, decisioning hooks, pre-contractual disclosure
Servicing: statements, repayment allocation waterfall, restructuring, arrears and collections states, IFRS 9 staging
"Split this transaction" — post-purchase instalments triggered from a settled card authorisation
Dependencies & build notes
CCD2 brings BNPL, interest-free instalments and sub-€200 loans fully into scope from 20 November 2026, with mandatory creditworthiness assessment and standardised disclosure
Many incumbent BNPL providers are not CCD2-ready — a compliance-native engine is a timing advantage, not just a feature
EMIs cannot lend from e-money funds; the module must support a separate funding entity or partner lender
Credit bureau and open-banking affordability adapters are buy, not build. Note the EU AI Act treats creditworthiness scoring as high-risk — keep the human decision point
💡
Scope decision: Phase 2 — flagship module, CCD2-native. Time this to the November 2026 CCD2 application date and market it as compliance-by-construction. This is where your loan management systems background is a genuine moat against Tuum and Mambu, whose lending engines are built for classic instalment loans rather than merchant-embedded credit.
FX & Remittance Game changer
Cross-border · Multi-currency · Payout networks
Remittance operators, migrant-focused EMIs, SME cross-border, marketplace payouts
Customer value
Send money home, priced transparently
Quote-to-delivery cross-border transfer: a locked rate and total cost shown up front, corridor-aware routing, and payout to a bank account, wallet, card or cash pickup — with the recipient's experience as good as the sender's.
Commercial value
FX spread is the business model
Unlike every other module here, the revenue is the spread, not a fee. A platform that manages the rate, the margin and the corridor cost per transaction directly controls your customer's gross margin — which justifies premium pricing and makes the module extremely sticky.
Functional scope
Rate and margin engine: multiple rate sources, configurable markup by corridor, customer segment, amount band and channel; rate locks with expiry; promotional and first-transfer pricing
Quote-to-settlement lifecycle: firm quote with total cost and delivery estimate, FX booking, deal capture, hedging position, and settlement against the booked rate
Corridor and payout routing: pick the payout partner per corridor by cost, speed and reliability, with automatic failover and a normalised status model across very different partner APIs
Payout methods: bank account, mobile wallet, card push, cash pickup — each with its own data requirements and failure modes
Compliance: sender and beneficiary data to Transfer of Funds Regulation standards, purpose-of-payment capture, corridor-specific limits, sanctions screening on both parties
Treasury: pre-funded versus post-funded corridors, nostro position per currency, exposure limits, and reconciliation of partner settlement
Dependencies & build notes
Execution and payout reach are bought, not built: Currencycloud, Airwallex and specialist corridor partners cover the long tail (see Payment Processor Analysis)
The defensible part is the margin engine, the routing logic and the treasury view — nobody sells those as a core banking module, and every remittance operator builds them badly in-house
Pre-funding is the operational reality in most corridors; the ledger must model funds in transit at a partner as a distinct position, not as cash
Transparent total-cost disclosure is both a regulatory expectation and the competitive axis the whole sector now fights on
Shares the multi-asset ledger requirement with Loyalty and Stablecoin — and stablecoin rails are a live alternative for some corridors
💡
Scope decision: Phase 2 — licensable module, partner-executed. Remittance is one of the largest and least well-served EMI segments in Europe, and no core banking vendor in the competitive set offers a margin and corridor engine. The strategic appeal is that FX spread revenue grows with your customer's volume rather than their headcount, which means the module's value to them rises exactly as their ability to pay does.
Light Trade Finance Game changer
Business services · Working capital · Third-party funded
SME-focused EMIs and business-account propositions with import/export customers
Customer value
Working capital at the point of need
Invoice finance, purchase-order funding and supply-chain finance offered inside the business account, against invoices and orders the platform already holds. The SME never leaves the app, fills in no forms twice, and gets a decision in hours rather than weeks.
Commercial value
Introduction economics, not balance sheet
Delivered with a third-party provider: a specialist funder, invoice-finance platform or credit insurer carries the risk and the capital, your customer earns a referral or revenue share, and you licence the origination workflow. No lending licence, no capital, no credit risk on anyone's balance sheet that shouldn't have it.
Functional scope — deliberately "light"
Invoice finance / factoring: select an issued invoice, get an advance offer from the funding partner, assign the receivable, receive the advance into the business account, and settle automatically when the debtor pays into the invoice's vIBAN
Purchase-order and supplier finance: fund a confirmed order or pay a supplier early against an approved payable, with repayment scheduled to the buyer's terms
Document and milestone tracking: proforma invoice, order confirmation, shipping and delivery documents held against the trade, with status driving the funding lifecycle
Pre-qualification and data pack: the platform already holds the account history, the invoice, the debtor's payment behaviour and the KYB file — that data pack is what makes an hours-long decision possible
Explicitly out of scope: issuing letters of credit, bank guarantees and documentary collections. Those require a bank licence, confirmed-credit capacity and a correspondent network. Refer them to a partner bank; do not pretend to offer them
Dependencies & build notes
Requires a third-party funding partner from day one. Design the module as a marketplace with a partner-agnostic offer API, so a customer can plug in their own funder or insurer rather than yours
Depends on Invoicing and Collections — the invoice object and the per-invoice vIBAN are what make the advance self-liquidating. Without those this module has no foundation
Receivables assignment, notification and set-off are legally jurisdiction-specific; scope one or two markets first and take local advice rather than generalising
Where the end borrower is a consumer-adjacent micro-business, consumer credit rules may bite — keep the product strictly B2B
The risk to manage is reputational concentration: if the partner funder declines or fails, it is your customer's product that stops working. Contract for a second funder
💡
Scope decision: Phase 3 — module, partner-funded, explicitly scoped as "light". The reason this works is data, not capital: the platform already holds everything a funder needs to underwrite, which turns a traditionally paper-heavy product into an in-app decision. Keeping the heavy instruments out is what makes it buildable — and being honest in the naming about what it is not protects the proposition from an expectation it cannot meet.
Marketplace & split payments Game changer
Payments · Multi-party · Escrow
Marketplaces, gig platforms, travel, property, crowdfunding, PSP-adjacent EMIs
Customer value
Hold, split, pay out
Take one payment, hold it in escrow, split it across many sellers by rule, net off commission and refunds, and pay out on schedule — with each seller onboarded and KYB'd as a party.
Commercial value
Unlocks a new vertical
Marketplace flows are the single largest underserved EMI use case in Europe. Mangopay built a business on it; no core banking vendor offers it as a module.
Functional scope
Seller onboarding with KYB, tiered verification and per-seller wallet
Escrow/hold accounts with release conditions: delivery confirmation, time, manual, dispute hold
Split rules: percentage, fixed, tiered commission, multi-level (platform, sub-platform, seller)
Payout scheduling, netting, reserve and rolling-reserve handling, refund clawback
Dependencies & build notes
Client-money segregation rules make escrow legally delicate — the ledger model must satisfy safeguarding audit
Mass payout needs batch SCT Inst and strong beneficiary management with VoP at scale
Sellers may be in multiple jurisdictions — tax reporting (DAC7) obligations follow
Depends on the Business account party model, Onboarding KYB and Collections
💡
Scope decision: Phase 3 — vertical module. Strong proposition, but it changes your sales motion: marketplaces buy payments infrastructure, not core banking. Treat it as a deliberate second market entered once the EMI base is established, and validate with one design-partner marketplace before building.
Loyalty & card-linked offers Game changer
Engagement · Merchant-funded
Consumer wallets, retail co-brands, telco and retailer embedded finance
Customer value
Reasons to transact
Points, cashback and merchant-funded offers triggered from the authorisation stream, with rewards settling into the same wallet the customer spends from.
Commercial value
Merchant-funded revenue
The only proposition here funded by a third party rather than the end customer. For EMIs squeezed by interchange caps, merchant-funded rewards are the clearest new revenue line available.
Functional scope
Rules engine on the transaction stream: earn by MCC, merchant, amount, frequency, campaign window
Points ledger as a first-class currency on the same core ledger — earn, burn, expire, transfer
Card-linked offers: merchant funds the reward, settled post-clearing with reconciliation back to the merchant
Campaign management and performance reporting for the merchant side
Dependencies & build notes
Requires enriched merchant data (clean names, categories) — raw scheme data is not sufficient; plan for an enrichment provider
Points as a ledger currency has accounting and, in some jurisdictions, e-money implications — take advice early
Needs a merchant-side relationship your EMI customers may not have; consider partnering with an existing CLO network
Low technical risk, high go-to-market risk
💡
Scope decision: Phase 3 — module, partner-led. Build the rules engine and points ledger (cheap, reusable); integrate a third-party offers network rather than building merchant acquisition. The points ledger is worth building early regardless — it validates that your ledger genuinely supports arbitrary currencies.
Stablecoin & digital asset rails Game changer
Payments · MiCA · Crypto-fiat
Crypto-adjacent EMIs, cross-border remittance, CASP-licensed platforms
Customer value
Settlement without correspondents
On/off ramp between fiat balances and EMT stablecoins, with 24/7 settlement for cross-border flows that SWIFT cannot serve at the price point.
Commercial value
Optional, segment-specific
A premium module for a narrow but well-funded segment. Also a defensive feature: crypto-adjacent EMIs are routinely declined by generalist core vendors, making them a receptive audience.
Functional scope
Multi-asset ledger: fiat and EMT balances under one customer, with clear segregation
On/off ramp orchestration via licensed CASP partners; the platform never custodies keys
Travel Rule data capture and transmission on crypto transfers
Stablecoin settlement for cross-border payouts, with fiat conversion at the destination — a direct alternative rail for the FX & Remittance module
Dependencies & build notes
MiCA separates EMT issuance, custody and exchange into distinct authorisations — the platform must not blur them
Never hold keys. Orchestrate licensed custodians; this keeps you out of CASP scope
Note the AMLR sets a €1,000 CDD threshold for crypto-asset service providers — materially lower than the €10,000 general threshold
The multi-asset ledger requirement is the same one Loyalty and FX need — build once
💡
Scope decision: Phase 3 — optional module. Do not build for this segment first, but make the ledger multi-asset from the start so the option stays open at near-zero marginal cost. The same ledger capability serves points, FX and stablecoins.
Direct scheme participation Game changer
Payments · T2 / TIPS / RT1 / STEP2
Scaled EMIs and PIs ready to leave sponsored access behind
Customer value
Remove the intermediary
Since October 2025 non-bank PSPs can be direct participants in T2, TIPS, RT1 and STEP2-T. That removes sponsor fees, sponsor cut-offs and sponsor counterparty risk — the three things every scaled EMI complains about.
Commercial value
The retention story
Customers outgrow sponsors. A core that carries them from sponsored access to direct participation without re-platforming is a core they never leave. No analysed vendor markets this path.
Functional scope
Rail abstraction: the same internal payment object settles via sponsor API or via direct scheme connectivity
Intraday liquidity management: position monitoring, funding and defunding of the TIPS/T2 account, thresholds and alerts
Scheme-grade operations: reachability files, directory management, cut-offs, contingency and business-continuity procedures
Dual-run capability: operate sponsored and direct in parallel during migration
Dependencies & build notes
The TARGET Guideline amendment took effect in October 2025 after being postponed from April, once SFD and PSD2 amendments were transposed
Non-bank PSPs get no intraday credit facility and cannot use the TARGET account for safeguarding — liquidity planning is the real barrier
Most EMIs will not be ready for years; the platform capability is the sales story long before it is used
Highest operational-maturity requirement of anything in this document
💡
Scope decision: MVP — architecture; Phase 3 — capability. The abstraction belongs in the MVP because retrofitting it is a rewrite; the direct connectivity itself is a late deliverable. Of everything in this document this is the most defensible long-term position: the only core banking platform built for the EMI's whole lifecycle, from sponsored to self-sufficient.

Strategic direction

Bank-in-a-box, above the ledger. Every analysed vendor has a competent ledger. None sells onboarding orchestration, invoicing, receivables automation, FX margin management, trade finance, merchant-funded loyalty or AI-assisted migration as core banking modules. That is the whole opportunity.
Five architectural commitments must be made before the first module ships: multi-user party model, multi-asset ledger, processor abstraction for cards, rail abstraction for payments, and a 24/7 non-batch ledger. Each is cheap now and a rewrite later.
Sell the lifecycle, not the platform. Wallet → IBAN Account → Payments → Collections → Credit → Deposits, and sponsored access → direct scheme participation. Each step is a priced module and a reason not to leave.
Regulation sets the release calendar. Instant Payments reachability is already mandatory; CCD2 applies 20 Nov 2026; the EUDI Wallet must be offered by member states by end-2026 and accepted by relying parties by end-2027; the AMLR applies 10 Jul 2027; PSD3/PSR is expected in H2 2026 with roughly a 21-month clock, and it repeals both PSD2 and the E-Money Directive.

Explicit non-goals

Do not become a card processor or a BIN sponsor. Capital, PCI scope and scheme obligations are disproportionate. Integrate and abstract instead.
Do not acquire a licence. Vodeno and Solaris show the BaaS-plus-licence model is a different business with different economics. Software margins and licensed-entity margins do not mix well at your scale.
Do not take credit risk, and do not issue heavy trade instruments. Credit and trade finance are partner-funded by design. Letters of credit and guarantees need a bank — refer them out rather than approximating them.
Do not build KYC, KYB data, OCR, credit bureau, sanctions screening or the AI models. Orchestrate best-in-class providers and own the workflow, the audit trail and the feedback loop. Your customers already have preferences and contracts.
